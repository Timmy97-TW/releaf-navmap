The logic of a scene

A scene is not rearranged for the page. It is the valley with most of it taken away, so the same thing is always in the same company and the same kind of place.

One world, fixed places

Every thing has a zone and an order inside it: the village, the lab, the studio, the yard, the field, the town. A scene shows only the page's things, but in that order and on that ground, so the reactor is always right of the lab and left of the field, and the school is always in the town.

Things that belong together

When two things that go together are both in a scene, they are pulled side by side: the farmers' table at the field edge, the twin's screen against the reactor, the photometer beside the reactor's tray, the results board at the field, the notebook on the bench, the safety post at the lab door, the sculpture in front of the school.

Each group on its ground

Lab things stand on the lab floor under its roof, studio things under the studio roof, yard things on gravel behind a fence, the field in furrows, town things on the plaza, the village on its green. The ground tells a reader which part of the project they are looking at.

The page lights up

The page's own thing stands larger, under the pin, on a pool of light, and glows in the colour of its header the moment the scene scrolls into view. Pointing at anything else dims the scene to dusk and lights that thing with it, in the same colour.

Full frame, no words

The groups are scaled to fill the frame; nothing is written into the picture and nothing is listed under it. Point at a thing and its name appears in a small white callout.

Jacquelyn's pages, kept

Which pages appear in each scene is her choice from the valley prototype, unchanged. The zones, the order and the pairings are in scene.js, a few lines each, and the drawings are the same 27 the students are redrawing.
