The logic of a scene

Each scene is a sentence about the page: this came before it, this stands beside it, this comes after, and this comes back to it.

Before, middle, after

The road runs left to right in the order of the work, and nothing is drawn between the things: the light does the connecting. The Digital Twin's scene puts the Math Model to the left, because the equations were written before the system ran, and the Dry Lab Notebook, the Plants and the Results to the right, because they came after.

A peer at the same size

When a page mirrors another, the two stand together at one scale: the Digital Twin beside the reactor it imitates, the Math Model beside the twin that runs it, the reactor shed beside the reactor it multiplies.

The loop

Where the work learns from itself, pointing at the later thing makes its light breathe: results sharpen the twin's forecast, the model's fit, the next engineering cycle; the farmers' trials come back to Human Practices.

Set where the page lives

Wet-lab scenes happen under the lab pavilion, dry-lab scenes under the studio roof, project scenes in the gravel yard, engagement scenes on the town plaza, team scenes on the village green, the Plants scene in the field. A reader learns the sections by their ground.

Full frame, no words

The things are large and the floor fills the picture; nothing is written into it and nothing is listed under it. Point at a thing and its name appears in a small white callout; the title above says whose scene it is. The glow is always in the page's own colour, the colour of its header on the wiki.

Jacquelyn's pages, kept

Which pages appear in each scene is her choice from the valley prototype, unchanged. The order along the road, the peers and the loops are the new logic, written in scene.js as one line per page.
