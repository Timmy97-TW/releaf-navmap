The whole valley

The second picture above. All 27 pages stand in one landscape, each as the thing it is about, placed so that the valley tells the project's story from left to right. One road joins everything; the river crosses the field. Pointing at a place dims the valley to dusk and lights the place and every page linked to it, with the routes between them; the caption under the picture lists those links with their reasons.

The logic of the picture

Six zones along one road, in the order the work happened. A reader who knows where a page stands in the valley knows what kind of page it is.

TeamThe villageWhere the people are: their houses, the flag of the season, the notice board of who did what.
Wet LabThe labThe bench with its flasks, the rack of parts, the photometer, the safety post, the open notebook.
Dry LabThe studioThe whiteboard with the curve, the Digital Twin's screen, the peptide model, the laptop.
ProjectThe yardWhere the machine is built: the reactor, the gear bench, the reactor shed, the stage steps, the toolbox, the results board, and the ReLEAF sign at the road.
PlantsThe fieldThe crop rows with the farmers in them, and the survey map at the field edge.
EngagementThe townThe round table, the school, the market stall, the turbine, the council hall, the sculpture in the square.
Same drawings, two pictures

Each page has one drawing. The whole valley uses all 27 in their places; the page map borrows the ones that page links to and stands them along a road, so the two pictures teach the same vocabulary.

Light as the link

Dusk and light are the homepage's own language for the reactors. Here the lit places are the linked pages, and the routes between them are drawn on the ground, so a connection is seen, not inferred.

Drawn in code, after the painting

Everything is vector, built from her palette and her flat shapes with a dry-brush edge. It stays editable: a place can be moved, redrawn, or replaced by a hand-drawn layer at the same spot. AI-assisted, to be disclosed as such.
