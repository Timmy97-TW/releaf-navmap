Design logic

What changed from the valley, and why.

Direction, not decoration

Every related page is either something this page builds on or something it leads to. Left is before, right is after, the reader is in the middle. The arrows carry the reading order; nothing has to be decoded.

Every link says why

A name alone makes a reader guess. Under each page is one line saying what the connection is: "the runs these numbers come from", "the approvals before any sale". These are drafts for the page owners to rewrite.

One page is marked next

Six links is a menu. One of them wears a Read next tag, so a reader who does not want to choose still has a path through the wiki.

The wiki's own colours

Each card takes the colour the wiki already gives its section on the banner and the contents rail, and the icon the menu uses. A reader who has seen the menu recognises the section before reading the name.

Compact and flat

The map is about 300 pixels tall at desktop width, white ground, hairline cards. On a phone it becomes a list: you are here, builds on, leads to.

Jacquelyn's choices, kept

Which pages appear on which map is taken from her prototype unchanged (five or six per page). Only the lane, the phrase and the next-page tag were added. Her valley is kept in the repository for reference.

In a wiki page

Copy assets/navmap/ into the wiki's assets/, then add after a page's last section, before the footer. The page the reader is on is read from the URL; the Previous / Next row is removed unless data-keep-pagenav is set. Nothing is fetched from outside the site.

<link rel="stylesheet" href="../assets/navmap/navmap.css">
<section class="navmap"></section>                    <!-- the page map -->
<section class="navmap" data-map="all"></section>     <!-- the whole wiki, optional -->
<script src="../assets/navmap/navmap-data.js"></script>
<script src="../assets/navmap/navmap.js"></script>

Everything a page owner might change is in navmap-data.js:

FieldWhat it is
links[page]The related pages, in order. Add or remove one here and both maps follow.
lanein (this page builds on it, drawn on the left) or out (it leads there, drawn on the right).
whyThe one-line reason under the page's name. Lowercase fragment; the first letter is capitalised on screen.
nexttrue on exactly one out link per page: the Read next tag.
sections / pagesThe five menus and 27 pages with their colours, icons and captions, copied from the wiki's own nav. Slugs are iGEM's standard URLs and must not change.
